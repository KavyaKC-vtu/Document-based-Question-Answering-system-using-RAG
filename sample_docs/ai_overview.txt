Artificial Intelligence Overview

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is the simulation of human intelligence processes by machines, especially computer systems. These processes include learning (acquisition of information and rules for using it), reasoning (using rules to reach approximate or definite conclusions) and self-correction.

Machine Learning (ML) is a subset of AI that provides systems the ability to automatically learn and improve from experience without being explicitly programmed. Deep Learning is a subset of ML that uses neural networks with many layers (hence "deep").

Types of AI:
1. Narrow or Weak AI: Designed to perform a narrow task (e.g., voice assistants, image recognition).
2. General or Strong AI: A system with generalized human cognitive abilities.
3. Superintelligence: Hypothetical AI that surpasses human intelligence.

Key Applications:
- Natural Language Processing (NLP): Understanding and generating human language. Examples: chatbots, translation.
- Computer Vision: Enabling machines to see and interpret images.
- Robotics: Physical AI agents that interact with the world.
- Expert Systems: AI that mimics decision-making of human experts.

Challenges in AI include bias in training data, explainability (why did the model decide that?), energy consumption, and ethical concerns around automation and privacy.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is a technique that enhances Large Language Models (LLMs) by retrieving relevant documents from an external knowledge base before generating an answer. This reduces hallucination and keeps answers grounded in source data. RAG pipeline: Ingest -> Chunk -> Embed -> Store in Vector DB -> Retrieve top-k -> Generate with context.
